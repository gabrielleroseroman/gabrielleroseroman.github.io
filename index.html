About

Bridging science and policy

I’m a 2025 Yale Environmental Studies graduate based in Phoenix. I started in science, doing genomics research at the Smithsonian, but community conservation fieldwork in Kenya showed me I wanted to work with people as much as data. I now bring that analytical, science-grounded lens to policy and coordination.

As Scientist in Residence for the U.S.-Mexico Foundation for Science’s water program, I built a binational border water initiative from scratch. Working across the border is what drew me to water, since it is so clearly a shared resource. I’m also helping coordinate Arizona’s first statewide environmental convening with the Arizona Community Foundation.

I love breaking down silos between organizations, and I’m looking for a full-time role in water policy, conservation, or the broader environmental field in Arizona or the Southwest.

Report · U.S.-Mexico Foundation for Science · 2026

Border Water Report 2026

A 50-page assessment of how FUMEC can advance binational agricultural water conservation along the U.S.-Mexico border. It combines a federal and nonprofit programming review, a policy landscape assessment, a survey of water conservation technologies, and in-depth stakeholder interviews, and recommends four action areas, from a smart farming pilot to a binational water “digital twin.”

Presented to FUMEC’s Board of Directors and distributed at the “Bridges Over Water” binational conference (Monterrey, Mexico, Oct 2026). Full report coming soon on FUMEC’s website.

Senior thesis · Yale University, 2025

For This Land, God Is Red: Vine Deloria Jr., Indigenous Worldviews, and Challenging Western Thought

This thesis examines the reception and enduring significance of Vine Deloria Jr.’s God Is Red (1973), a groundbreaking critique of Western Christianity and defense of Indigenous worldviews. Drawing on archival research at Yale’s Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library and interviews with leading Indigenous scholars, it traces how the book was received in its time and how its influence on debates about Indigenous sovereignty and environmental ethics has grown over fifty years.

Advised by Professor Gerald Torres. Full text available on request.
